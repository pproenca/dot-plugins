### Autopilot-full

**You own the verdicts, never the PRs. One owner runs each PR from build to merge, and nothing merges without your clean swarm verdict.** For "autopilot this queue", "full autopilot", and one-owner-per-PR programs. The job is a queue of independent PRs handed over to drive to merged with full autonomy. Orchestrate runs a standing program whose coordinator lands verified work itself and whose workers never merge; here each PR's owner carries the whole lifecycle through the merge, and the root keeps only verification, countersigns, and audits.

1. **Mark the operator's items and honor state-then-wait.** Items the operator names stay hers. She reviews and clicks, and no owner merges one. When she asks for the protocol or plan, deliver it and stop. Execution starts only on her explicit go. On that go, create a goal with the full program objective. The goal continues across turns until the queue is done.
2. **Create worktrees, then spawn one owner per PR with the full lifecycle.** Codex collaboration agents share the parent's checkout by default. Create one explicit git worktree and branch per PR, pass the absolute path to its owner, and require every owner command to use that working directory. One collaboration agent per PR owns build, Graphite registration, proof on the real artifact through **prove-it-works**, skeptical automated-review triage per `../references/bugbot-triage.md`, diff cleanup through **unslop**, **no-comments**, a restack onto current trunk, the babysit loop, and the merge itself. The restack always precedes babysit and never waits for drift or conflicts. Every owner keeps a local `decisions.tsv` trail per **show-me-your-work**. Never commit the trail. Return it with the owner's reports. The merge is the one step an owner may not take alone. Step 4 gates it.
3. **Run owners in true parallel and never stack.** Many owners run at once only when they have separate worktrees, branches, and disjoint file ownership. Cross-PR drift is absorbed by rebase. Only genuinely overlapping work serializes. Self-contained PRs branch straight off main, and sequenced work is merge-then-branch. Every PR is still gt-registered; the Graphite-metadata rule is about the UI, not stacks. One exception: an owner that must split a genuinely dependent change may hold a short private stack.
4. **Swarm-verify every merge-ready head before its merge.** At the owner's merge-ready head SHA, fan out parallel independent verifiers per **swarm** and aggregate one verdict. The fan-out mechanics live there. Do not restate them. Re-run the gates at that SHA. Prove the load-bearing behavior through the available browser, computer-use, or CLI control skill. Audit the receipts and diff without trusting the PR body. The live lane is the floor. A verdict without it is not clean. No merge proceeds without the root's clean verdict. Findings go back to the owner for a fix. A new head gets a fresh swarm and verdict.
5. **On a clean verdict the owner merges and takes the next item.** The owner merges only from a head freshly restacked on trunk. The merge-ready report is made at a trunk-current head, and the swarm verdict pins that SHA. If trunk moves again before the merge, the patch-id rule in `playbooks/shipping.md` governs re-verification; a new head voids the verdict unless the patch-id is unchanged. The owner squash-merges its own PR and picks up its next self-contained item from the queue. The operator's full-autonomy grant plus the root's clean verdict is the merge authorization that babysitting alone never has. Operator-named items stop at merge-ready and wait for her click.
6. **Run the root layer.** A genuinely new raise of a pinned gate or budget value needs a fresh countersign, granted only after verifier proof. Absorbing values that already landed on trunk is drift, not a raise. Run an audit tick over all owners roughly every 30 minutes through a Codex thread heartbeat automation. Never leave the cadence to memory or completion notifications. At each tick, re-read this playbook from the active pstack skill path, then re-read the active goal. Audit the operation against both. Fix drift during that tick and treat it as urgent. Check every owner's current status and collect the decision trails. Count only side effects as progress: commits, pushes, PR or check changes, and reports. Treat a lane that passes its expected runtime without a side effect as stuck. Stop its current turn when interruption is supported, then dispatch a fresh replacement. Do not wait for a polite return. When merges batch, run a retro pass and a post-merge bot-comment sweep.
7. **Stand down instantly on the operator's stop.** Her hold or stand-down reaches every owner as a zero-writes order immediately. Owners hold their briefs until she releases them.

**Reply:** the queue with each PR's owner, state, and head SHA; each verdict and the swarm that produced it; what merged and what each owner took next; countersigns granted and why; open operator gates; where the collected decision trails live.
